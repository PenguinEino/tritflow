# TritFlow: Ternary HDL to GDS with the Open-Source TR-1um PDK

Large language models with weights restricted to −1, 0 and +1, such as BitNet b1.58, replace multiplications in matrix products with additions and subtractions. This renews the motivation for circuits that compute directly on ternary data. These computations nevertheless run on binary circuits. Much of the research on direct ternary logic uses devices not in volume production, such as CNTFETs and memristors, or remains at the simulation stage. Research on ternary RTL synthesis produces gate netlists but does not address layout or fabrication. Open CMOS tools implement ternary values through binary encoding. To our knowledge, no design flow from ternary HDL to GDS on a manufacturable open-source PDK has been reported.

This notebook implements that flow as TritFlow on OpenSUSI TR-1um, the first open-source PDK released by a commercial foundry in Japan. TritFlow turns the author's taped-out ternary circuits into standard cells and integrates ternary HDL, logic synthesis, place and route, PDK DRC/LVS, and post-layout simulation. It uses balanced ternary: each digit is −1, 0 or +1, with positional weights that are powers of 3 (1, 3, 9, …). The corresponding circuit voltages are −5 V, 0 V and +5 V.

The ternary circuits underlying TritFlow's standard cells have already been taped out on Japan's first OpenMPW service shuttle (OpenSUSI-TR10, 2026), which uses TR-1um as an open-source PDK. The chips are due in January 2027. If selected, the author will present TritFlow at ISSCC in February together with the first measurements of its underlying cells, then fabricate a circuit designed with TritFlow on the next OpenSUSI shuttle. Completing this cycle of design, fabrication, measurement and reporting will demonstrate the three Code-a-Chip values through TritFlow and TR-1um, an open-source semiconductor platform originating in Japan.

**Reproducibility.** Subsequent researchers should be able to understand, reconstruct and explain TritFlow. Its cell circuits, logic synthesis algorithms, and place and route algorithms are kept simple enough to follow while producing GDS that passes PDK DRC/LVS and is suitable for tapeout. This notebook explains each stage and makes it executable in place. Because the cells are based on circuits being fabricated and measured, the explanations can also be checked against silicon measurements (chapters 1–5).

**Reusability.** That understanding provides a basis for extending TritFlow. Editing the HDL produces a custom ternary circuit through the same flow, including GDS and an MPW frame for shuttle submission. The simple structure also allows cells to be added and synthesis or place and route algorithms to be replaced. The cells, flow and notebook are released under Apache-2.0 as a starting point for subsequent research and shuttle submissions (chapters 6 and 7).

**Education.** The progression from understanding to extension also provides a learning path. Every stage runs interactively in the notebook and presents figures and quantitative results, allowing newcomers to logic design to follow the full path from HDL to GDS. The same flow connects to fabrication and measurement.
