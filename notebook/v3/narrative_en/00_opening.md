TritFlow is a design flow from ternary HDL to GDS on OpenSUSI TR-1um, built on CMOS cells that handle three voltage levels directly. The running example is dot4, a circuit that computes the four-term dot product of ternary weights w<sub>i</sub> and inputs x<sub>i</sub>, y = w<sub>0</sub>x<sub>0</sub> + … + w<sub>3</sub>x<sub>3</sub>. This notebook takes dot4 through logic synthesis, two-metal place and route, DRC/LVS, post-layout simulation and ternary timing analysis, then edits its HDL and reruns the same flow.

TritFlow uses balanced ternary: each digit is −1, 0 or +1, with positional weights that are powers of 3 (1, 3, 9, …). The corresponding circuit voltages are −5 V, 0 V and +5 V. Balanced ternary is also the number system of [Setun](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-22816-2_10), the ternary computer built at Moscow State University in 1958. In the linear layers of [BitNet b1.58](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2402.17764), weights restricted to these three values select the input, its negation or zero, allowing dot products to use additions and subtractions (the inputs are 8-bit). This renews the motivation for circuits that compute directly on ternary data. Existing implementations encode these weights in binary hardware. Ternary logic circuits have been studied extensively in CNTFET, memristor and CMOS technologies (for example, [CNTFET](https://doi.org/10.1109/TNANO.2009.2036845), [memristor](https://doi.org/10.1109/TCSI.2020.3027693), [CMOS](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41928-019-0272-8)). Design tools, however, either stop at [gate netlists](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2025.3597293) or implement ternary values with binary signals ([MRCS](https://github.com/aiunderstand/MixedRadixCircuitSynthesis/tree/0865cee1f49fe9a803b4910b570f4e96e514c61b), [Takahe](https://github.com/Zaneham/takahe/blob/e7123ea60269922acb283d576678a3aabe51f4f4/designs/setun70_physical.sv), [Ankhdjet](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2608.26206)). To our knowledge, no flow from ternary HDL to GDS on a manufacturable open-source PDK using CMOS cells with native three-voltage signals has been reported.

This notebook implements that flow as TritFlow on [OpenSUSI TR-1um](https://github.com/OpenSUSI/TR-1um/tree/5d963db9d44e192d37a0489c208bfccf2e2f690c), an open-source PDK released by a commercial foundry in Japan. TritFlow turns [ternary logic circuits taped out by the author](https://github.com/PenguinEino/opensusi-tr10-balanced-ternary-logic/tree/8a60b97868cad008ff21fefca5da13dcd06063f8) (OpenSUSI-TR10, 2026) into standard cells, and takes a dedicated ternary HDL through logic synthesis, place and route, PDK DRC/LVS and post-layout simulation in a single flow.

The OpenSUSI-TR10 chips arrive in January 2027; if selected, the author will present TritFlow at ISSCC in February together with the first measurements of its cells. A circuit designed with TritFlow will be fabricated on the next OpenSUSI shuttle. Completing this cycle of design, fabrication, measurement and reporting with TritFlow will demonstrate that TR-1um is capable enough to deliver the three values Code-a-Chip promotes. Through this cycle, the project aims to substantially advance the democratization of chip design in Japan.

**Reproducibility.** Subsequent researchers should be able to understand, reconstruct and explain TritFlow. Its cell circuits, logic synthesis algorithms, and place and route algorithms are kept simple enough to follow while producing core GDS verified by PDK DRC/LVS and post-layout SPICE. This notebook explains each stage and makes it executable in place. Because the cells are based on circuits being fabricated and measured, the explanations can also be checked against silicon measurements (chapters 1–5).

**Reusability.** That understanding provides a basis for extending TritFlow. Editing the HDL regenerates a verified core through the same flow. For dot4, the notebook also shows the top-level layout in an MPW frame. The simple structure also allows cells to be added and synthesis or place and route algorithms to be replaced. The cells, flow and notebook are released under Apache-2.0 as a starting point for subsequent research and shuttle submissions (chapter 6).

**Education.** The progression from understanding to extension also provides a learning path. Every stage runs interactively in the notebook and presents figures and quantitative results, allowing readers who know basic CMOS logic and an HDL to follow the full path from HDL to GDS. The same flow connects to fabrication and measurement.
